Scientific claim verification systems often struggle to balance retrieval speed and reasoning quality when processing large evidence collections. In this study, we introduce LiteVerifier, a lightweight transformer-based architecture for classifying whether scientific claims are supported, contradicted, or not addressed by retrieved abstracts. The model uses a two-stage pipeline combining dense retrieval with cross-encoder reranking and was evaluated on a benchmark of biomedical and computer science claims. LiteVerifier achieved comparable accuracy to larger baseline models while reducing inference latency by 38% and GPU memory usage by 41%. These findings suggest that efficient claim verification systems can improve access to trustworthy scientific information in real-time research support tools.
